From the lab folder to your report

You build the report in a copy of your lab folder, with the same structure and the same key steps as the labs: the same do-files, in the same order, edited to fit your own question. You do not start from scratch, and you do not add new files next to the lab’s. The lab do-files are a model, not the answer: they show how; the instructions say what your report must do, and they ask for more than the lab demo did (see step 3).

1. Copy. Make a copy of the whole project folder (Mac: select it and press Cmd+D; Windows: copy and paste) and rename the copy na310g-<your code>. Leave the lab folder as it is, to look back at.

2. Clear out the labs, in the copy. Delete:

  • lab1-catchup, lab2-catchup, check_lab1.do, check_lab2.do, ai_task_lab1 and ai_task_lab2;
  • everything inside data/processed, output/logs and output/tables (old logs, tables and data files: master.do rebuilds them from your own data);
  • in data/raw, the course dataset na310g_data_course.dta. Put your own na310g_data_<your code>.dta there instead, unchanged. Then change the use line in 1_load_check.do to your file’s name (step 3), or master.do stops with “na310g_data_course.dta not found”.

Keep master.do, the folder dofiles, README and report_checklist.

3. Adapt each do-file to your question, and do what the instructions ask, not only what the lab did:

  • 1_load_check.do: the use line reads your own file.
  • 2_sample_vars.do: your industry (B1), geography (B2), outcome and controls (B3), and the sample steps (B5). Screen the data more carefully than the lab demo did: for each variable, how many firm-years are negative, zero or missing, what you do with them and why (instructions, section 5, “About variables in general”), and every step from the original file to your final sample (section 9, item 4).
  • 3_descriptives.do: all variables in your final analysis, and the table’s title and note.
  • 4_regressions.do: your outcome in every regression and in the table. Keep one table, the three-column one (pooled OLS, fixed effects, random effects); delete the Lab 1 table if it is still there.
  • 5_tests_iv.do: the tests with your outcome. The IV part (G) only if you argue for an instrument.
  • In each file, update the header (PURPOSE, CREATED) and make the comments say why you do each step.

4. The occupation file is optional. Your research question needs no merge: your outcome, ai_firm and the required controls are all in your own dataset. You need na310g_occupations_ssyk2012.dta only if you use an occupation variable, for example as an instrument (for a VG). If you do not, delete block B4 (the merge) in 2_sample_vars.do, block G in 5_tests_iv.do, and the occupation file in data/raw.

5. Run everything from the copy. Close Stata, then double-click the copy’s master.do: it sets $root to the folder Stata opens in, so if Stata is still open from the lab folder, your files write there. master.do must run from top to bottom without an error. Its log, with the verification line, goes in your appendix.

Getting the most out of it

  1. Run your do-file from the top first. Many problems disappear when the file runs in one go.
  2. Bring the smallest piece of code that shows the problem, and the error message.
  3. Say what you expected to see before you ran it. That is usually where the misunderstanding is.

Common questions

Where do I work on my report, so I do not overwrite the lab files? In a copy of the lab folder, made and cleaned as in From the lab folder to your report above.

Professional services is J + K + M. Does the lab’s grouping include M (divisions 69–75)? Yes. The line in 2_sample_vars.do reads inrange(division, 58, 66) | inrange(division, 69, 75); | means “or”, so it covers J and K (58–66) and M (69–75). Use it as it is.

Should I drop the firms outside my industry and geography, and before or after I make my variables? Yes: keep only your industry and your geography (keep if industry == ..., drop if inlist(ser_laen, ...)). For a variable computed row by row, such as vacancies per employee, the order makes no difference to its values. Still, select the sample first and count after each step: section 4 of the report shows the selection step by step, from the original N to your final N. Count also the firm-years that get a missing value (for example zero employees), since they drop out of the regression.

Some values of a variable I want to log are negative. Drop them or keep the variable in levels? Both can be defended; the brief asks you to choose, report how many firm-years it concerns, and say why. Logs are usual for skewed measures such as value added per employee: then look at the firm-years you lose before you drop them (how many firms, the same firms again and again, how they differ in size, industry and year) and report it. Levels keep every firm-year but give a skewed variable, read per krona. Strongest: one as your main model, the other as a robustness check. And if it is a control, ask whether it is built from the same accounts as your outcome (value added and net sales both start from sales): part of their correlation is then mechanical.
